# Kev vs Jev on bench-linguist

Generated by `python3 benchmarks/bench-linguist/analysis/kev_vs_jev.py`. Both models ran the same protocol — the file's content only, GitHub Linguist's 804 languages asked in 4 seeded groups of ~200 (with "none of these"), then a final among the groups' winners — Jev 1.13 through OpenRouter, Kev-4B (an open decision model, Q8_0) locally with llama.cpp. Failure reports: [Kev](../FAILURES-kev-linguist.md), [Jev](../FAILURES-jev-linguist.md).

Kev is right on 62.5 % of the 3,404 files, Jev on 82.3 %. **Jev is right and Kev wrong on 747 files**; the reverse on 74; both right on 2053, both wrong on 530.

## 5. Experiment: is it the option order?

On a fixed sample (`kev_order_experiment.py`, seed 20261003) of 100 files Jev gets right and Kev wrong, and 50 files both get right, Kev is asked again in three ways: the same groups in reverse order; 16 groups of ~50 options instead of 4 of ~200; and PL-ultimate-llm's candidates for the file's extension first (as `tools/jev_cascade.py`, file name not shown). Right answers:

| variant | of the files Jev gets right and Kev wrong | of the files both get right |
|---|---:|---:|
| as run (4 groups of ~200) | 0/100 | 50/50 |
| same groups, reverse order | 20/100 | 46/50 |
| 16 groups of ~50 | 31/100 | 43/50 |
| the extension's candidates first | 59/100 | 48/50 |

With the order reversed, the first options of the original order (Ecere Projects, Move) disappear from Kev's wrong answers, and the last ones come in — the order, not the content, moves the answer: YAML (5; listed 102 of 201 as run, 100 reversed), Toit (5; listed 201 of 201 as run, 1 reversed), JavaScript (4; listed 131 of 201 as run, 71 reversed), SQL (3; listed 59 of 201 as run, 143 reversed).

The candidates first recover most of the gap: with a handful of options, Kev's primacy bias has little room. Smaller groups help less, and reversing the order trades errors for others.

## What it means

- Kev's gap to Jev is mostly in the first round: with ~200 options per question it favours the options listed first and seldom answers "none of these", so the wrong language often wins a group and the final gets crowded. The decision model is not the limit by itself — the protocol, long option lists, is.
- For a small local decision model, ask few options: the extension's candidates first (the cascade) recovers 59 of the 100 sampled files Jev gets right and Kev wrong, keeping 48 of 50 controls; smaller groups recover 31, reversing the order 20.
- Jev shows no such bias: its group winners are spread almost evenly over the positions.
